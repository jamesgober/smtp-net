# smtp-net v0.5.0 - Feature complete

**Date:** 2026-11-04
**Compare:** [`v0.4.0...v0.5.0`](https://github.com/jamesgober/smtp-net/compare/v0.4.0...v0.5.0)

**DKIM, modern AUTH, and the pieces a busy service needs.** v0.5.0 signed mail with DKIM (Ed25519 and RSA), added SCRAM-SHA-256 and XOAUTH2 to AUTH, and brought the connection pool, the observer hook, and streamed attachments. Every item the specification grades P0 to P2 was present.

## What's in 0.5.0

- **DKIM.** `DkimSigner` signed with Ed25519-SHA256 (RFC 8463) and RSA-SHA256 (RFC 8301), relaxed or simple canonicalization, the default header list with oversigning (a header added in transit broke the signature), `t=`, optional `x=` and `i=`, never `l=` or `rsa-sha1`. Several signatures stacked in the order given, each over the original message. `ServerBuilder::dkim` signed everything `send` sent; `Message::prepare_signed` signed before a job was stored; `PreparedMessage::sign` signed raw bytes. Keys loaded from PKCS#8 or PKCS#1, PEM or DER, lived inside crypt-io's types, and were wiped on drop; RSA keys under 2048 or over 4096 bits were refused with their size. `DkimKey::dns_record` gave the TXT record. The canonicalizers built in every configuration, `no_std` included.
- **SCRAM-SHA-256** (feature `scram`). The password never left the client; the server nonce had to extend the client's, the iteration count stay within 4,096 to 1,000,000, and the server prove it held the key with a signature checked in constant time, or the message was not sent. It came first in the default mechanism list; credentials outside printable ASCII fell through to PLAIN until SASLprep exists.
- **XOAUTH2.** `Credentials::oauth2` sent a token to Gmail or Microsoft 365; on failure `Error::auth_detail` gave the server's status (`"401"` for an expired token) and nothing else of its error document was kept.
- **Custom mechanisms.** The `Mechanism` trait and `CustomMechanism` let a caller add a SASL mechanism; the session handled base64, the line limit, and the step bound.
- **Pool.** `Pool` shared at most 64 connections across threads, claimed by a lock-free bitmap (checked with loom), dropped failed connections, replaced idle ones on use, and failed with `PoolExhausted` (temporary) past its wait limit.
- **Observer.** `ServerBuilder::observer` received connection, TLS, reply-code, delivery, failure, and security events, never secrets, bodies, or reply text.
- **Streamed attachments.** `Attachment::from_source` pulled content from a `BodySource` (or any `std::io::Read` through `ReadSource`) while the message was serialized, with the declared length enforced to the octet.
- **Dependencies.** tls-net 0.5 (from 0.3) and crypt-io 1.3, both on aws-lc-rs 1.19.
- **Docs.** `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and `docs/DELIVERABILITY.md` (SPF, DKIM, DMARC, reverse DNS, bulk sender rules, bounces, rate limits); examples `send_with_dkim` and `dkim_dns_record`.

## Verification

- RFC 8463 Appendix A reproduced byte for byte (Ed25519 signature and body hash) and the RFC 7677 SCRAM exchange as a transcript; the empty-body hashes of RFC 6376.
- Every signature checked in CI by dkimpy, an independent verifier: Ed25519, RSA 2048 and 4096, PKCS#8 and PKCS#1, all four canonicalization pairs, a multipart message, two signatures on one message; copies changed after signing (an added Subject, appended text) failed as they should.
- 68 transcripts whole and split into 1 to 16 octet pieces, including SCRAM's failure paths (wrong server signature, a success without proof, nonce not extended, iterations 4,095 and 1,000,001) and XOAUTH2's error document; end-to-end tests against `TestServer` for SCRAM, XOAUTH2, the pool (64 threads on one connection), the observer, and streamed attachments.
- Fuzz targets `dkim` and `sasl` added (eleven in all); loom models for the pool's slots.
- CI: Linux, macOS, Windows on stable and 1.85; bare-metal `no_std` with and without `scram`; interop with Mailpit and Exim.

## Not in 0.5.0

- `allow_rsa_1024`: crypt-io signs only with RSA keys of 2048 bits or more, which is what RFC 8301 asks for.
- DKIM results at Gmail and Outlook.com need a sending domain with the keys in its DNS; that check moved to the 0.8 field test.
