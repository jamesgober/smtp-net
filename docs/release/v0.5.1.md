# smtp-net v0.5.1 - Shorter encoded headers

**Date:** 2026-11-05
**Compare:** [`v0.5.0...v0.5.1`](https://github.com/jamesgober/smtp-net/compare/v0.5.0...v0.5.1)

**The RFC 2047 B form.** parse-net 0.9.1 published the B form of encoded words, and smtp-net 0.5.1 used it: a header whose text is mostly not ASCII, such as a Japanese or Russian subject or display name, went out as `=?UTF-8?B?...?=` words when that was shorter than the Q form, about 40% shorter for Japanese, with fewer folded lines. Mostly ASCII text, such as `Café au lait` or `José`, kept the readable Q form.

## Changed

- `MessageBuilder::subject`, other unstructured headers, display names, and `encode_word` chose between Q and B per header (D-082). The meaning of the header did not change, only its length.
- parse-net 0.9.1 became the minimum.

## Verification

- Unit tests for Japanese, Russian, mostly ASCII, and lookalike subjects: the expected form, the shorter length, a round trip through parse-net's `decode_words`, 75-octet words, and 78-column lines; the existing transcripts, DKIM corpus, and dkimpy check unchanged.
