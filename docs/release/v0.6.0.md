# smtp-net v0.6.0 - Optimisation

**Date:** 2026-11-06
**Compare:** [`v0.5.1...v0.6.0`](https://github.com/jamesgober/smtp-net/compare/v0.5.1...v0.6.0)

**Measured and tuned.** Every performance target in the plan was measured, against lettre, mail-builder, mail-send, and mail-auth as well as on its own, and the results are in [`docs/PERFORMANCE.md`](../PERFORMANCE.md). The hot paths now scan eight octets at a time, base64 runs 1.7 times faster and quoted-printable about 2.7 times faster, a message build allocates 21 times instead of 97, and starting a transaction allocates nothing. No public API was removed or changed in a breaking way; no check was relaxed and the crate still forbids `unsafe` code.

## Highlights

- One message on a reused connection takes 24 µs at a 0 ms round trip and 2.1 ms at 1 ms, against 67 ms and 101 ms for lettre and 65 ms and 96 ms for mail-send: smtp-net pipelines MAIL, RCPT, and DATA and disables Nagle's algorithm.
- Building and serializing a verification email takes about 3.5 µs, 1.8 times faster than lettre; with a 1 MiB attachment, 3.1 times faster.
- An Ed25519 DKIM signature of a 10 KiB message takes about 20 µs (30 at 0.5), RSA-2048 about 220 µs (lettre: 3.2 ms).
- Linux is the production target: crypt-io's Ed25519 is several times slower under Windows MSVC.

## Changed

- Dot-stuffing, raw-message input, the header value check, and relaxed DKIM body canonicalization scan eight octets at a time with `u64` arithmetic. Each scan is checked against the loop it replaced at every offset (D-086).
- base64 encodes six octets per step through a 12-bit table; quoted-printable copies literal runs through a class table.
- A text plus HTML build allocates 21 times (97 at 0.5): header text is shared, written names are static, the builder's strings move into the message, and boundaries are inline (D-083).
- `Envelope` keeps its addresses behind an `Arc`: a clone is a reference count and `Session::begin` allocates nothing (D-084).
- EHLO capabilities keep the AUTH mechanism names in one buffer.
- `TestServer` sets `TCP_NODELAY`.
- net-io 0.6.1 is the minimum.

## Added

- `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`.
- Test support: `Fault::RoundTrip(Duration)`, one delay per client turnaround (D-089).
- Benchmarks `dkim`, `send`, and `gate`, and `benches/examples/targets.rs`, which prints every target in one table.

## Targets not met

Recorded with their numbers and reasons in `dev/PLAN.md` (D-083, D-085, D-093) and re-measured on a quiet machine in the 0.9 pass:

- Build allocations: 21 against an estimate of 8. Every remaining allocation is a value the message owns.
- Session memory: 19,176 bytes against 16 KiB, because of the 16 KiB default output buffer. With a 4 KiB buffer a session is 6,888 bytes.
- Under heavy load on the shared build machine, five rows came in short of their targets: quoted-printable, single-line reply parsing, dot-stuffing, EHLO with capabilities, and relaxed body canonicalization. The first three met their targets in a quieter run during the work. Both sets of numbers are in `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`.

## Verification

- Every scan and encoder matches the code it replaced over a 3,000-case corpus and at every offset; the transcripts, split at every byte, the DKIM corpus with the dkimpy check, and the Exim and Mailpit interop jobs are unchanged.
- `tests/alloc.rs` holds the allocation counts: 21 for the build, none for `Session::begin`, the body path, positive replies, the encoders, the header gate, and `write_to`.
