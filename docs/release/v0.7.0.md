# smtp-net v0.7.0 - Hardening

**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Compare:** [`v0.6.0...v0.7.0`](https://github.com/jamesgober/smtp-net/compare/v0.6.0...v0.7.0)

**Hardened.** Every row of the threat table now has a passing test, mapped in the new [`docs/SECURITY.md`](../SECURITY.md). Every fuzz target ran for an hour with no finding. The 24-hour soak passed, and the SCRAM signature comparison passed a timing check. The work found one real defect, fixed below. Two new defences ship: refusal of bidirectional overrides, on by default, and a recipient budget per domain.

## Highlights

- An out-of-step reply could dead-letter a good message. When a server sent one reply too many under pipelining, a `250` could end up matched to DATA, and smtp-net reported a permanent `Rejected`. It is now a temporary protocol error, the session closes, and after the end of data the message is possibly delivered.
- `DomainLimit` caps the recipients at any one domain per hour, so a compromised account or an abused form cannot flood one victim domain. Pairs of gates combine, for example `.gate((HourlyLimit::recipients(500), DomainLimit::per_domain(100)))`.
- Bidirectional overrides and isolates in the Subject and display names are refused by default (`Invoice \u{202E}fdp.exe`). An opt-in policy refuses display names that look like addresses.

## Fixed

- A reply whose class cannot answer its command was taken as a refusal: a `250` to DATA, or a `354` to MAIL or to the end of data. It is now `Protocol(UnexpectedReply)`, temporary and flagged as a security event, and the session closes. After the end of data the message is possibly delivered (D-096).

## Added

- `docs/SECURITY.md`: scope, assumptions, the 36 threats with their defences, tests, and costs, the hardening record, what is not defended, and operating guidance.
- `MessageBuilder::allow_bidi_controls` and `refuse_address_like_display_names`; `HeaderError::BidiControl` and `AddressLikeDisplayName` (T-25, T-35, D-097).
- `DomainLimit` (feature `std`); `SendGate::admit_envelope` and `refund_envelope`, default methods, so existing gates are unchanged; `(A, B)` as a gate (D-098).
- Test support: `TestServerBuilder::record(false)` and `TestServer::messages`.
- `tests/soak.rs`, `tests/threats.rs`, three transcripts, a self-signed certificate test, peak-memory tests for reply floods and huge MIME trees, a dudect-style SCRAM timing check, and a `loom` CI job.

## Changed

- Bidi controls are refused by default, as above. Right-to-left text and the RLM and LRM marks pass.
- The `prepared_decode` fuzz target frames half its inputs with a valid header and CRC-32C (D-101).
- parse-net 0.9.2 is the minimum: MIME parameters are folded by its `write_mime_param_at`; the output is unchanged (D-104).

## Verification

- Fuzzing: every one of the eleven targets ran an hour with no crash, leak, or timeout. `prepared_decode` ran a second hour with framed inputs (100.3 M executions, coverage 615, from 52); `session_server_bytes` and `transcript` ran again with the reply-class check, and `address` and `content_type` with parse-net 0.9.2, all clean. Figures per target are in [`docs/SECURITY.md`](../SECURITY.md).
- Soak (NFR-14), 24 hours on commit `1d089f4` (the parse-net change after it leaves the output byte for byte the same): 8,640,000 messages over one reused connection through `(HourlyLimit, DomainLimit)`, 3,277,232 through a pool of two, and 86,400 sends to the ten fault servers, every one ending as expected. Every message reported sent reached the server. The highest live heap was 620 KB in the first third and 673 KB in the last, against a 1 MiB limit; descriptors stayed at 29 and threads at 21. 709,825 sends started after their 10 ms slot because the machine was shared (load average above 40); the test counts them and does not fail on them.

- SCRAM timing (T-29): Welch's t between wrong signatures that differ in the first or the last octet stayed between -1.76 and 0.07 on Linux and at most 1.19 on Windows, against dudect's 4.5 (D-099).
- Every threat test, transcript (split at every byte), property test, the DKIM corpus with dkimpy, and the Exim and Mailpit interop jobs pass on Linux, macOS, and Windows, stable and 1.85.
